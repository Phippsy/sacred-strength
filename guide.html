Conversation guide

Everything needed to hold 8 to 10 good conversations. Use it loosely: the best moments come from following what she says.

Step 1

Who to ask

List 10 to 12 women aged roughly 30 to 40, so you end up with 8 to 10 conversations. A mix matters more than the number.

Cycle

Regular cycles

Women who know roughly when their period is due.

Cycle

Irregular or hard to predict

Including anyone who doesn't track at all or isn't sure.

Cycle

On hormonal contraception

The pill, implant, injection or hormonal coil. They may have no natural cycle.

Life stage

Approaching perimenopause

Late 30s, maybe noticing changes already.

Movement

Regular exercisers

Gym, classes, running or yoga most weeks.

Movement

Not exercising much

Or has fallen off and wants to get back to it.

Friends of friends often give more honest answers than close friends. If you know anyone who has come off weight-loss injections, one or two of them would help test that idea too.

Step 2

The invitation

Copy, change anything that doesn't sound like you, and send.

Email or longer message

Hi [name], I'm working on an idea for women's fitness and wellbeing, built around how we actually feel through the month. Before I build anything, I want to hear from real women about their experience. Would you be up for a 30 to 45 minute chat over a coffee (or a video call) in the next few weeks? There are no right answers and nothing to buy. I'd just love to hear how things are for you. Everything stays between us and I won't use your name. Heather x

WhatsApp

Hi [name]! I'm shaping an idea for women's fitness and wellbeing and want to hear from real women first. Fancy a 30 minute coffee and a chat in the next few weeks? Nothing to buy, no right answers, all confidential. Heather x
Step 3

Before you start

A few lines to open with, so she feels relaxed and knows what to expect.

Opening
  • Thank her. "Thanks so much for doing this."
  • Say why. "I'm shaping something for women's fitness and wellbeing, and I want to start from real experiences."
  • Take the pressure off. "There are no right answers, and I'm not selling anything."
  • Make it safe. "Some of this is personal. Skip anything you'd rather not talk about."
  • Ask about notes. "Is it OK if I jot a few notes? I won't write your name down."

Listen more than you talk

Aim for her talking 80% of the time. Silence is fine; she'll often fill it with the good stuff.

Ask about real moments

"Tell me about the last time..." gets better answers than "Would you...?"

Hold your idea back

Describe Sacred Strength at the end, if at all. Once she's heard it, her answers bend towards it.

Follow her energy

If something clearly matters to her, stay there. Ask "why?" and "what happened next?"

Step 3, continued

Questions

You won't get through all of them, and you don't need to. Spend most of the time on part 2.

Part 1

Her life and movement

Warm up with easy, everyday questions.

  1. Tell me a bit about a normal week for you. Where does exercise or movement fit, if at all?
  2. What have you tried for fitness over the years? What stuck, what didn't, and why?
  3. How do you feel about the fitness world and all the advice out there?
Part 2

How she feels through the month

The heart of the conversation. Give it the most time.

  1. Do you notice changes in how you feel across the month? Energy, mood, strength, sleep, appetite?
  2. Tell me about the last time you felt really strong or full of energy. And the last time you felt flat. Did you know why?Real moments tell you more than general opinions.
  3. How do those changes affect your week, your work or your training?
  4. When your energy is low, what do you usually do? Push through, rest, feel guilty?
Part 3

Tracking

Some women will have a lot to say here, others nothing. Both are useful.

  1. Do you track your cycle, or anything else about your health? What with?If yes: why did you start, and what do you actually do with it?
  2. Is your cycle fairly regular, or harder to predict?Only if she's comfortable. Move on if not.
  3. How would you feel about sharing that kind of information with a coach?
Part 4

What would help

Hold back on describing your idea. Let her describe what she'd want.

  1. If you had someone helping you with fitness and wellbeing, what would you want from them?
  2. Would you rather be told what to do, or be given options and choose?
  3. What would make you trust an online coach?
  4. Have you ever paid for a PT, a class or an app? What made it worth it, or not?
  5. Imagine it's six months from now and something has really helped. What's different?
Part 5

Closing

  1. Is there anything I didn't ask that I should have?
  2. Who else do you think I should talk to?
  3. Would you like to hear about it if I try something out?
Step 4

Notes, straight after

Ten minutes while it's fresh. Write her words, not your summary of them.

Notes template

Conversation number: Date: About her (no name): age range, cycle situation, how she exercises now How she feels through the month, in her words: What she tracks, with what, and why: What gets in the way of looking after herself: What she'd want from a coach: Plan or options? Has she paid for fitness before? Would she pay for this? For what? Words or phrases worth keeping: What surprised me: Would she like to try something early? Yes / No / Maybe
Keep it private

This is health information

Number the conversations instead of using names, and keep notes somewhere only you can see. If she asks to see what you wrote, show her.

Watch for

Things that come up again and again

Feelings, frustrations and phrases you hear from three or more women are the ones to pay attention to.

Keep track

Tell Donal as you go

Send a quick message after each one, and the counter on the home page gets updated.

Step 5

Afterwards: review and decide

Once 8 conversations are done, Heather and Donal spend an hour going through the notes together.

1

How do they feel?

What did most women say about the month? Which words did they use?

2

Does tracking matter?

Do they track already? Would they share it? Does the cycle matter to them as much as we think?

3

What do they want?

Plan or options? Coach, content or community? What would they pay for?

4

Who's keen?

Who asked to hear more or try something? They're the first testers.

Then choose the next step

These are the likely options. What we hear may suggest something else entirely, and that's fine.

Most likely

A small tester group

5 to 10 women try a simple daily check-in for one cycle, and Heather sends a weekly note.

Possible

Reshape the idea

If what women want differs from what we expected, rework the offer and talk to a few more.

Possible

Pick a niche

If one group stands out, such as women in perimenopause or after weight-loss jabs, focus there first.
